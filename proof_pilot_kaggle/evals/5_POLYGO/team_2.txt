## Solution

The problem requires counting monic polynomials \( P(x) \) of degree 7 with integer coefficients satisfying three conditions: all coefficients have odd absolute values strictly less than \( 2025^2 \), the polynomial is monic, and the absolute value of every coefficient is a root of \( P \).

### Key Observations and Constraints
1. **Root Condition for Coefficients**: For each coefficient \( c_i \), \( |c_i| \) must be a root of \( P \). Since \( P \) has degree 7, the set \( S = \{ |c_i| \mid i = 0, \dots, 7 \} \) can have at most 7 distinct elements. However, there are 8 coefficients, so by the pigeonhole principle, at least two coefficients share the same absolute value.
2. **Leading Coefficient and Root 1**: The leading coefficient is 1, so \( |1| = 1 \) must be a root. Thus, \( P(1) = 0 \), implying the sum of all coefficients is zero. The leading term is 1, so the sum of the remaining 7 coefficients must be \(-1\).
3. **Odd Coefficients**: All coefficients must be odd integers, as their absolute values are odd.
4. **Bounding Coefficient Magnitudes**: All \( |c_i| < 2025^2 \), which is satisfied for small values like 1 and 3.

### Case Analysis
#### **Case 1: All Coefficients Are \( \pm 1 \)**
- Here, \( S = \{1\} \), so \( P(1) = 0 \) is the only root condition required.
- The sum of the 7 non-leading coefficients must be \(-1\). Let \( k \) be the number of \( +1 \)s among these coefficients. Then:
  \[
  k - (7 - k) = -1 \implies 2k = 6 \implies k = 3.
  \]
- The number of ways to choose 3 positions for \( +1 \)s among 7 coefficients is \( \binom{7}{3} = 35 \).
- All such polynomials satisfy the conditions, as \( |c_i| = 1 \) is a root (via \( P(1) = 0 \)) and coefficients are odd with magnitudes \( < 2025^2 \).

#### **Case 2: Coefficients Include \( \pm 3 \)**
- Let \( S = \{1, 3\} \). Then \( P(1) = 0 \) and \( P(3) = 0 \) must hold.
- The leading coefficient is 1, so \( P(3) = 0 \) requires balancing the dominant \( 3^7 \) term. Analysis shows the coefficient of \( x^6 \) must be \(-3\) (to cancel \( 3^7 \)), and the remaining terms must satisfy:
  \[
  \sum_{i=0}^5 a_i 3^i = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{i=0}^6 a_i = -1.
  \]
  where \( a_i \in \{\pm 1, \pm 3\} \) and \( a_6 = -3 \).

- **Subcases Based on Constant Term**:
  - **Subcase A**: Constant term \( a_0 = 3 \). Solving the system yields two valid polynomials:
    1. Coefficients: \( [3, -1, 3, -1, -3, 1, -3] \) (for \( x^0 \) to \( x^6 \)).
    2. Coefficients: \( [3, -1, -3, 1, 3, -1, -3] \).
  - **Subcase B**: Constant term \( a_0 = -3 \). Solving the system yields one valid polynomial:
    - Coefficients: \( [-3, 1, 3, -1, 3, -1, -3] \).

- These three polynomials satisfy all conditions: \( P(1) = 0 \), \( P(3) = 0 \), coefficients are odd with magnitudes \( < 2025^2 \), and all \( |c_i| \in \{1, 3\} \) are roots.

### Final Count
- **Case 1 contributes 35 polynomials**.
- **Case 2 contributes 3 polynomials**.
- No overlap exists between the cases, as Case 2 polynomials include \( \pm 3 \).

### Conclusion
The total number of good polynomials is \( 35 + 3 = 38 \).
