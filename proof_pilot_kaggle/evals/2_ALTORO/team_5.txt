We are given triangle \(ABC\) with side lengths \(AB = 26\), \(BC = 17\), \(AC = 25\).  We set up a coordinate system with \(B = (0,0)\), \(C = (17,0)\), and solve for \(A\).  From the distances we find \(A = (10,24)\).  The orthocenter \(H\) is the intersection of the altitudes; since \(BC\) is horizontal, the altitude from \(A\) is the vertical line \(x = 10\).  The altitude from \(B\) is perpendicular to \(AC\); its equation is \(24t = 10\) giving \(t = \frac{5}{12}\), so \(H = \left(10, \frac{35}{12}\right)\).

The feet of the altitudes are
\[
D = (10,0),\qquad 
E = \left(\frac{9792}{625},\frac{2856}{625}\right),\qquad 
F = \left(\frac{425}{169},\frac{1020}{169}\right).
\]

Line \(EF\) meets \(BC\) (the \(x\)-axis) at \(X\).  Using the two‑point form we obtain
\[
X = \left(\frac{170}{3},0\right).
\]

The circumcircle \(\Omega\) has centre \(O = \left(\frac{17}{2},\frac{253}{24}\right)\) and radius \(R = \frac{325}{24}\).

Line \(AX\) is parametrised as \(A + s\,(X-A)\).  Substituting into the equation of \(\Omega\) gives a quadratic in \(s\).  Besides \(s=0\) (point \(A\)) we obtain
\[
s = \frac{759}{12392}.
\]
Hence the second intersection \(Q\) of \(AX\) with \(\Omega\) is
\[
Q = \left(10 + 140\cdot\frac{759}{12392},\; 24 - 72\cdot\frac{759}{12392}\right)
   = \left(\frac{57545}{3098},\;\frac{30345}{1549}\right).
\]

The nine‑point circle (circle \(DEF\)) has centre \(N = \left(\frac{37}{4},\frac{323}{48}\right)\) and radius \(R_N = \frac{325}{48}\).  A well‑known homothety with centre \(H\) and factor \(\frac12\) sends the circumcircle to the nine‑point circle.  Therefore the image of \(Q\) under this homothety lies on the nine‑point circle and on the segment \(HQ\).  That image is exactly the midpoint of \(H\) and \(Q\); call it \(P\).  Thus
\[
P = \frac{H+Q}{2}.
\]

Because \(P\) is the midpoint of \(HQ\), we have \(PQ = \frac12 HQ\).  The required ratio is
\[
\frac{PQ}{PE} = \frac{HQ}{2\,PE}.
\]

We now compute \(HQ^2\) and \(PE^2\) using the coordinates.  Let \(d = 1549\) (note that \(3098 = 2d\)).  Then
\[
Q = \left(\frac{57545}{2d},\;\frac{30345}{d}\right),\qquad 
H = \left(10,\;\frac{35}{12}\right).
\]

\[
\Delta x = Q_x-H_x = \frac{57545-20d}{2d},\qquad 
\Delta y = Q_y-H_y = \frac{364140-35d}{12d}.
\]

With \(d=1549\) we get \(57545-20d = 26565\) and \(364140-35d = 309925\).  Hence
\[
HQ^2 = \frac{26565^2}{4d^2} + \frac{309925^2}{144d^2}
      = \frac{36\cdot26565^2 + 309925^2}{144d^2}.
\]

Evaluating the squares:
\[
26565^2 = 705\,699\,225,\qquad 
309925^2 = 96\,053\,505\,625,
\]
\[
36\cdot26565^2 = 25\,405\,172\,100,\qquad 
\text{sum} = 121\,458\,677\,725.
\]

Thus
\[
HQ^2 = \frac{121\,458\,677\,725}{144\,d^2}.
\]

Factorising the numerator shows it is \(1549 \times 78\,411\,025\).  Since \(d=1549\), we obtain
\[
HQ^2 = \frac{78\,411\,025}{144\,d}.
\]

But \(78\,411\,025 = 8855^2\) (because \(8854^2 = 78\,393\,316\) and \(8855^2 = 78\,393\,316+17\,709 = 78\,411\,025\)).  Therefore
\[
HQ^2 = \frac{8855^2}{144\,d}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 
PQ^2 = \frac{HQ^2}{4} = \frac{8855^2}{576\,d}.
\]

Now we compute \(PE^2\).  Because \(P\) and \(E\) lie on the nine‑point circle, we use the relation
\[
PE^2 = 2R_N^2 - 2\,\overrightarrow{NP}\cdot\overrightarrow{NE}.
\]

In the scaled system (multiplied by \(48\) to clear denominators) we have
\[
N' = (444,323),\qquad 
P' = \left(240+\frac{690540}{d},\;70+\frac{728280}{d}\right),\qquad 
E' = \left(\frac{470016}{625},\;\frac{137088}{625}\right).

The dot product \(\overrightarrow{NP}\cdot\overrightarrow{NE}\) in the original coordinates is
\[

The squared radius is \(R_N^2 = \left(\frac{325}{48}\right)^2 = \frac{105625}{2304}\).  Hence
\[
PE^2 = 2\cdot\frac{105625}{2304} - 2\cdot\frac{50\,312\,467\,283}{625\,d\cdot 2304}
      = \frac{211250}{2304} - \frac{100\,624\,934\,566}{625\,d\cdot 2304}.

Writing the first term with denominator \(625d\cdot2304\) gives

PE^2 = \frac{211250\cdot625d - 100\,624\,934\,566}{625d\cdot2304}.

Compute \(211250\cdot625 = 132\,031\,250\).  Then
