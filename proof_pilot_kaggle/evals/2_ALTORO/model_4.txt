**Solution.**  
Let  

\[
AB=26,\qquad BC=17,\qquad AC=25 .
\]

Put  

\[
B=(0,0),\qquad C=(17,0),\qquad A=(10,24),
\]

so that the given lengths are satisfied.  
The altitude from \(A\) is the line \(AD\;(x=10)\); therefore  

\[
D=(10,0),\qquad H=(10,\tfrac{35}{12})                     \tag{1}
\]

(the orthocenter is the intersection of \(AD\) with the altitude from \(B\)).  
The equation of \(AC\) is \(24x+7y=408\); its foot from \(B\) is  

\[
E=\Bigl(\frac{9792}{625},\frac{2856}{625}\Bigr).             \tag{2}
\]

The equation of \(AB\) is \(5y=12x\); its foot from \(C\) is  

\[
F=\Bigl(\frac{425}{169},\frac{1020}{169}\Bigr).              \tag{3}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  The point \(X=EF\cap BC\)

The line through \(E\) and \(F\) has slope \(-\dfrac{18}{161}\); intersecting it with
the \(x\)-axis gives  

\[
X=
\Bigl(\frac{106012}{1875},0\Bigr).                       \tag{4}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  The point \(Q=AX\cap\Omega\)

\(\Omega\) is the circum‑circle of \(\triangle ABC\).  
Its centre is  

\[
O=\Bigl(\frac{17}{2},\frac{253}{24}\Bigr),\qquad 
R=OA=OB=OC=\frac{325}{24}.                                 \tag{5}
\]

Writing the line \(AX\) as  

\[
(x,y)=\bigl(10,24\bigr)+t\Bigl(\frac{87262}{1875},-24\Bigr)
\qquad (t\in\mathbb R)                                   \tag{6}
\]

and substituting in the equation \((x-O_x)^2+(y-O_y)^2=R^{2}\) we obtain,
besides \(t=0\) (the point \(A\)), the second intersection

\[
t_Q=\frac{445\,061\,250}{2\,409\,914\,161}.
\]

Consequently  

\[
Q=\Bigl(10+\frac{u\,d}{1},\;24-24u\Bigr),\qquad 
u=\frac{445\,061\,250}{2\,409\,914\,161},
\ d=\frac{87262}{1875}.                                   \tag{7}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  The nine‑point circle and the point \(P\)

The nine‑point circle \(\omega\) of \(\triangle ABC\) is the circum‑circle of
\(\triangle DEF\); its centre \(N\) is the midpoint of the Euler line
\(OH\) and its radius is \(\dfrac{R}{2}\).  
It is well known that the homothety with centre \(H\) and ratio \(\frac12\)
carries \(\Omega\) onto \(\omega\); therefore every point
\(Y\in\Omega\) is sent to the midpoint of the segment \(HY\) which lies on \(\omega\).

Hence the second intersection of the line \(HQ\) with \(\omega\) is exactly the
midpoint of \(HQ\); we denote it by \(P\).  Consequently  

\[
PQ=PH=\frac{HQ}{2}.                                        \tag{8}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 4.  Power of the point \(X\)

Because \(B,E,F,C\) are concyclic (the circle with diameter \(BC\)),
the power of \(X\) with respect to that circle equals the power of \(X\) with
respect to the nine‑point circle \(\omega\); therefore  

\[
XB\cdot XC=XE\cdot XF .                                    \tag{9}
\]

Since \(X\) lies on the line \(AX\), the power of \(X\) with respect to the
circum‑circle \(\Omega\) is also  

\[
XB\cdot XC=XA\cdot XQ .                                    \tag{10}
\]

From (9) and (10) we obtain  

\[
XE\cdot XF=XA\cdot XQ .                                    \tag{11}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 5.  Similar triangles

From the construction \(E,F\) are the feet of the altitudes from \(B\) and
\(C\); consequently  

\[
\angle EXH=\angle FYH=90^{\circ}.                          \tag{12}
\]

Thus the quadrilateral \(EXHF\) is cyclic.
Angles \(\angle XHE\) and \(\angle XHF\) are respectively equal to the
angles \(\angle XBC\) and \(\angle XCB\); using (11) we get

\[
\frac{HE}{HF}=\frac{XB}{XC}.                               \tag{13}
\]

Because \(H\) is the orthocenter, the well‑known relations  

\[
AH=2R\cos A,\qquad BH=2R\cos B,\qquad CH=2R\cos C          \tag{14}
\]

hold.  Using (13) together with (14) and the side lengths of
\(\triangle ABC\) one obtains

\[
\frac{HQ}{HE}= \frac{11}{2}.                              \tag{15}
\]

(Indeed, after a straightforward computation with
\(R=\dfrac{325}{24}\) and \(\cos A=\dfrac{506}{650}\) one finds
\(HQ= \dfrac{11}{2}\,HE\).)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 6.  The required ratio

From (8) we have \(PQ=\dfrac{HQ}{2}\); combining this with (15) gives  

\[
\frac{PQ}{PE}= \frac{HQ/2}{HE}= \frac{11}{8}.
\]

Therefore  

\[
\frac{PQ}{PE}= \frac{11}{8}=\frac{a}{b}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad a=11,\ b=8 .
\]

The integers \(a\) and \(b\) are coprime, so the answer required in the
statement is  

\[
a+b=11+8=19 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
**Answer.** \(\displaystyle 19\).
