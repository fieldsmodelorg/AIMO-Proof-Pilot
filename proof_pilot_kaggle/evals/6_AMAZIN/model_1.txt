Let $ABC$ be the acute triangle with $\angle CBA = 75^{\circ}$. We are given the orthocenter $H$ and circumcircle $\Omega$. The points $H_A$ and $H_C$ are the second intersections of lines $AH$ and $CH$ with $\Omega$. 

A key property of the orthocenter is that the reflection of $H$ across the sides of the triangle lies on the circumcircle. Specifically, $H_A$ is the reflection of $H$ across $BC$, and $H_C$ is the reflection of $H$ across $AB$. This implies $BH_A = BH$ and $BH_C = BH$. 

Let $\Gamma$ be the circle centered at $B$ with radius $BH$. Since $BH = BH_A = BH_C$, the points $H, H_A, H_C$ lie on $\Gamma$. The point $X$ is defined as the intersection of $\Gamma$ with the line $BC$ on the opposite side of $B$ from $C$, so $X$ also lies on $\Gamma$. Thus, $X, H, H_A, H_C$ are concyclic on $\Gamma$.

The problem defines $n$-good triangles where $CP/CH = \sqrt{n}$. The value of this ratio depends on the shape of the triangle (specifically angle $C$). Let $C$ be the measure of $\angle BCA$. Through geometric analysis involving the positions of $P$ on $CH$ and the properties of the circle $\Gamma$, the ratio $CP/CH$ can be expressed as a function of $C$. 

The condition that $n$ is "amazing" requires that there exists a triangle with $CP/CH = \sqrt{n}$ such that $AQ$ and $H_C Q$ are integers. Since $AQ$ and $H_C Q$ scale linearly with the size of the triangle, the condition that they are simultaneously integers implies that their ratio $AQ/H_C Q$ must be a rational number for the triangle's shape. This condition restricts the possible shapes (angles) of the triangle, and consequently, the possible values of $n$.
