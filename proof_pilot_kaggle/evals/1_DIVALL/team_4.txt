Let \(a_1,\dots,a_k\) be distinct positive integers with product \(P\) and sum \(S<3\cdot10^6\).  
Define  

\[
g=\gcd\!\left(\frac{P}{a_i}+1 : 1\le i\le k\right).
\]

---

### 1.  Basic congruences  

For each \(i\), \(g\mid\frac{P}{a_i}+1\); hence \(\frac{P}{a_i}\equiv-1\pmod g\).  
Multiplying by \(a_i\) gives \(P\equiv-a_i\pmod g\).  
Thus all \(a_i\) are congruent modulo \(g\).  
Let \(r\) be the common residue, \(0\le r<g\); then  

\[
a_i=r+g\,c_i\qquad (c_i\text{ distinct non‑negative integers}).
\]

If \(r=0\) then each \(a_i\) is a multiple of \(g\).  
Since \(k\ge3\), \(\frac{P}{a_i}\) is a multiple of \(g\), so \(\frac{P}{a_i}+1\equiv1\pmod g\) and therefore \(g=1\).  
Hence for any interesting case we have \(r\ge1\).

For any \(i\),  

\[
\frac{P}{a_i}=\prod_{j\ne i}a_j\equiv r^{\,k-1}\pmod g.
\]

Because \(\frac{P}{a_i}\equiv-1\pmod g\), we obtain  

\[
r^{\,k-1}\equiv-1\pmod g. \tag{1}
\]

---

### 2.  The sum bound  

The three smallest positive numbers in the residue class \(r\pmod g\) are \(r,\;r+g,\;r+2g\) (since \(r\ge1\)). Their sum is \(3r+3g\).  
The actual sum \(S\) is at least this sum, so  

\[
3r+3g<3\cdot10^6\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r+g<10^6. \tag{2}
\]

---

### 3.  The case \(k\ge4\) cannot beat the maximum  

For \(k\ge4\) the smallest \(k\) numbers in the residue class are \(r,\;r+g,\dots,\;r+(k-1)g\); their sum is  

\[
k r + g\cdot\frac{k(k-1)}{2}.
\]

Because \(S\) is at least this sum,

\[
k r + g\cdot\frac{k(k-1)}{2} < 3\cdot10^6.
\]

For \(k=4\) this gives \(4r+6g<3\cdot10^6\), i.e. \(2r+3g<1.5\cdot10^6\).  
Since \(r\ge1\), we obtain \(3g<1.5\cdot10^6-2\), so \(g\le499\,999\).  
For larger \(k\) the coefficient of \(g\) is even larger, so \(g\) is even smaller.  
Hence every configuration with \(k\ge4\) satisfies \(g\le499\,999\).  
Therefore the overall maximum must be attained for \(k=3\).

---

### 4.  The case \(k=3\)  

For \(k=3\) equation (1) becomes \(r^2\equiv-1\pmod g\); hence  

\[
g\mid r^2+1. \tag{3}
\]

Write \(r^2+1 = g\,t\) with \(t\in\mathbb N\).  
Together with (2) we have  

\[
r + \frac{r^2+1}{t} < 10^6. \tag{4}
\]

We now maximise \(g\) under (3) and (4).

---

### 5.  Maximising \(g\)  

We consider the possible values of \(t\).

* **\(t=1\)**: \(g = r^2+1\).  
  Condition (4) becomes \(r^2+r+1<10^6\).  
  The largest integer \(r\) satisfying this is \(r=999\), giving \(g=999^2+1=998\,002\).

* **\(t=2\)**: \(g = \dfrac{r^2+1}{2}\).  
  Condition (4) becomes \(r+\dfrac{r^2+1}{2}<10^6\;\Longleftrightarrow\; (r+1)^2<2\cdot10^6\).  
  Since \(1414^2=2\,002\,225>2\cdot10^6\) and \(1413^2=1\,996\,569<2\cdot10^6\), we have \(r+1\le1414\), i.e. \(r\le1413\).  
  Moreover \(r\) must be odd (because \(r^2+1\) is even). The largest odd \(r\le1413\) is \(r=1413\).  
  Then  

  \[
  g = \frac{1413^2+1}{2}= \frac{1\,996\,569+1}{2}=998\,285.
  \]

* **\(t\ge3\)**: We show that \(g\le998\,285\).  
  Suppose, for contradiction, that \(g\ge998\,286\).  
  From (2) we have \(r<10^6-g\le1714\), so \(r\le1713\).  
  Then \(r^2+1\le1713^2+1=2\,934\,570\).  
  Since \(t\ge3\),  

  \[
  g = \frac{r^2+1}{t}\le\frac{r^2+1}{3}\le\frac{2\,934\,570}{3}=978\,190.
  \]

  This contradicts \(g\ge998\,286\). Hence \(g\le998\,285\) for every \(t\ge3\).

Thus the largest possible \(g\) for \(k=3\) is at most \(998\,285\), and we already have a construction achieving this value.

---

### 6.  Construction attaining \(g=998\,285\)  

Take \(r=1413\) and \(g=998\,285\).  
Set  

\[
a_1=1413,\qquad a_2=1413+998\,285=999\,698,\qquad a_3=1413+2\cdot998\,285=1\,997\,983.
\]

These are distinct positive integers. Their sum is  

\[
1413+999\,698+1\,997\,983=2\,997\,094<3\cdot10^6.
\]

Now compute:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{P}{a_1}+1 &= a_2a_3+1 = (r+g)(r+2g)+1 = r^2+3rg+2g^2+1 \\
               &= (r^2+1)+g(3r+2g)=g t + g(3r+2g)=g(t+3r+2g),\\[2mm]
\frac{P}{a_2}+1 &= a_1a_3+1 = r(r+2g)+1 = r^2+2rg+1 = (r^2+1)+2rg = g t + 2rg = g(t+2r),\\[2mm]
\frac{P}{a_3}+1 &= a_1a_2+1 = r(r+g)+1 = r^2+rg+1 = (r^2+1)+rg = g t + rg = g(t+r).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(t = \dfrac{r^2+1}{g} = \dfrac{1413^2+1}{998\,285}= \dfrac{1\,996\,570}{998\,285}=2\).  
Thus  

\[
\frac{P}{a_1}+1 = g(2+3\cdot1413+2\cdot998\,285)=g\cdot2\,000\,811,\qquad
\frac{P}{a_2}+1 = g\cdot2\cdot1414 = g\cdot2828,\qquad
\frac{P}{a_3}+1 = g\cdot(2+1413)=g\cdot1415.
\]

Since \(\gcd(2828,1415)=1\) (because \(2828=2\cdot1414\) and \(1415\) is odd and coprime to \(1414\)), the three numbers have greatest common divisor exactly \(g\).  
Hence \(g=998\,285\) is attainable.

---

### 7.  Conclusion  

The largest possible value of \(g\) over all choices of \(k\ge3\) and distinct positive integers \(a_i\) is  

\[
\boxed{998285}.
\]
