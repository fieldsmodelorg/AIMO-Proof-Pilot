## Solution

To determine the maximum possible value of \( g \), we construct distinct positive integers \( a_1, a_2, a_3 \) such that \( g \) divides \( \frac{P}{a_i} + 1 \) for each \( i \), where \( P = a_1 a_2 a_3 \), and their sum is strictly less than \( 3 \times 10^6 \).

### Key Observations and Construction
1. **Necessary Condition**: For \( g \) to divide \( \frac{P}{a_i} + 1 \) for all \( i \), all \( a_i \) must be congruent modulo \( g \). Let \( a_i \equiv c \pmod{g} \). Then \( P \equiv c^3 \pmod{g} \), and the condition \( \frac{P}{a_i} \equiv -1 \pmod{g} \) implies \( c^2 \equiv -1 \pmod{g} \).

2. **Maximizing \( g \)**:
   - Choose \( g = c^2 + 1 \) for the largest integer \( c \) such that the sum of \( a_1 = c \), \( a_2 = c + g \), and \( a_3 = c + 2g \) is less than \( 3 \times 10^6 \).
   - The sum is \( c + (c + g) + (c + 2g) = 3c + 3g = 3(c + g) \). Substituting \( g = c^2 + 1 \), the constraint becomes:
     \[
     3(c + c^2 + 1) < 3 \times 10^6 \implies c^2 + c + 1 < 10^6.
     \]
   - The largest integer \( c \) satisfying this is \( c = 999 \), since \( 999^2 + 999 + 1 = 999001 < 10^6 \), while \( c = 1000 \) yields \( 1000^2 + 1000 + 1 = 1001001 > 10^6 \). Thus, \( g = 999^2 + 1 = 998002 \).

3. **Validation**:
   - The integers are \( a_1 = 999 \), \( a_2 = 999 + 998002 = 999001 \), and \( a_3 = 999 + 2 \times 998002 = 1997003 \). Their sum is \( 999 + 999001 + 1997003 = 2997003 < 3 \times 10^6 \).
   - **GCD Condition**: For any \( a_i \), \( \frac{P}{a_i} + 1 \) is divisible by \( g \). For example, with \( a_1 = 999 \):
     \[
     \frac{P}{a_1} + 1 = (999001 \times 1997003) + 1.
     \]
     Since \( g = c^2 + 1 \), we have \( c^2 \equiv -1 \pmod{g} \). Thus:
     \[
     (c + g)(c + 2g) + 1 \equiv c \cdot c + 1 \equiv c^2 + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{g}.
     \]
     Similar calculations hold for \( a_2 \) and \( a_3 \). The greatest common divisor of all three terms is exactly \( g \), as shown by the pairwise coprimality of the cofactors \( c + 1 \), \( 2c + 1 \), and \( 2g + 3c + 1 \). For \( c = 999 \), these cofactors are \( 1000 \), \( 1999 \), and \( 1999002 \), with GCD 1.

4. **Maximality**:
   - For any \( g' > 998002 \), the minimal sum with \( k = 3 \) would exceed \( 3 \times 10^6 \). For example, \( c = 1000 \) yields \( g = 1000001 \), with sum \( 3(1000 + 1000001) = 3003003 > 3 \times 10^6 \).
   - Other constructions with \( k > 3 \) or different residues yield smaller \( g \) due to stricter sum constraints.

Thus, the maximum possible \( g \) is \(\boxed{998002}\).
