To determine the maximum possible value of \( g \) across all valid choices of \( a_1, \ldots, a_k \) with \( k \geq 3 \), we analyze the conditions imposed by the problem. The key requirement is that \( g \) divides each \( \left( \frac{P}{a_i} + 1 \right) \) for all \( i \), where \( P = a_1 a_2 \cdots a_k \). This implies \( \frac{P}{a_i} \equiv -1 \pmod{g} \) for each \( i \), leading to the congruence \( P \equiv -a_i \pmod{g} \) for all \( i \). Consequently, all \( a_i \) are congruent modulo \( g \), say to \( r \), and \( r \equiv -P \pmod{g} \). Substituting \( P \equiv r^k \pmod{g} \) yields \( r \equiv -r^k \pmod{g} \), or \( r(1 + r^{k-1}) \equiv 0 \pmod{g} \). Since \( r \not\equiv 0 \pmod{g} \), we require \( 1 + r^{k-1} \equiv 0 \pmod{g} \).

To maximize \( g \), we consider \( k = 3 \), as larger \( k \) would restrict \( g \) further due to the sum constraint. For \( k = 3 \), the condition becomes \( r^2 \equiv -1 \pmod{g} \), necessitating that \( -1 \) is a quadratic residue modulo \( g \). This requires \( g \) to be a product of primes \( \equiv 1 \pmod{4} \) (or 2, but 2 cannot contribute significantly). We seek the largest \( g < 5 \times 10^5 \) (to ensure the sum \( 3(r + g) < 3 \times 10^6 \)) composed of such primes.

The primes \( \equiv 1 \pmod{4} \) less than \( 5 \times 10^5 \) include 5, 13, 17, 29, 37, 41, 53, 61, 73, 89, 97, etc. Testing combinations, the product \( 5 \times 17 \times 61 \times 97 = 502,\!945 \) is the largest under \( 5 \times 10^5 \). We verify \( -1 \) is a quadratic residue modulo each prime factor and construct \( r \) via the Chinese Remainder Theorem. For \( g = 502,\!945 \), choosing \( r \equiv 414,\!362 \pmod{502,\!945} \) satisfies \( r^2 \equiv -1 \pmod{g} \). The numbers \( a_1 = 414,\!362 \), \( a_2 = 917,\!307 \), \( a_3 = 1,\!420,\!252 \) have sum \( 2,\!751,\!921 < 3 \times 10^6 \), and each \( \left( \frac{P}{a_i} + 1 \right) \) is divisible by \( 502,\!945 \).

No larger \( g \) meets all conditions. Thus, the maximum \( g \) is \( \boxed{502945} \).
