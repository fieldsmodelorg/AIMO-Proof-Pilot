We first note that after the stick is cut at all marked points, the pieces are positive lengths summing to \(1\).  The players then take turns choosing a piece; each wants to maximise his own total.  A standard greedy argument shows that the optimal strategy for both players is always to take the largest remaining piece.  Consequently, if the pieces are sorted in non‑increasing order  
\[
a_1\ge a_2\ge\cdots\ge a_k,\qquad\sum_{i=1}^k a_i=1,
\]  
the first player (Liu) obtains the sum of the odd‑indexed pieces  
\[
V=a_1+a_3+a_5+\cdots .
\]  
Thus the problem reduces to: Liu chooses a set \(L\) of at most \(n\) points on the stick, then Xia chooses a set \(X\) of at most \(n\) points disjoint from \(L\).  The stick is cut at all points of \(L\cup X\); let the pieces be sorted as above.  Liu’s total is \(V(L\cup X)\).  He wants to maximise the minimum of \(V\) over all admissible \(X\); denote this value by \(c(n)\).

---

### 1.  Lower bounds

**For all \(n\).**  If Liu marks no points, then after Xia’s marks the pieces are determined by \(X\).  Sort them as \(a_1\ge a_2\ge\cdots\ge a_k\).  From \(a_{2i-1}\ge a_{2i}\) we obtain  
\[
a_1+a_3+\cdots\ge a_2+a_4+\cdots .
\]  
Adding the two inequalities gives \(2(a_1+a_3+\cdots)\ge 1\), so the odd‑sum is at least \(\frac12\).  Hence \(c(n)\ge\frac12\) for every positive integer \(n\).

**For \(n=1\).**  Liu marks the point \(\frac13\).  The stick is cut into two pieces of lengths \(\frac13\) and \(\frac23\).  
If Xia marks no point, Liu receives the whole stick, i.e. \(\frac23\).  
If Xia marks a point, he must split either piece.  Splitting the smaller piece \(\frac13\) gives three pieces \(\frac23,\,y,\frac13-y\); the odd‑sum is \(\frac23+\min(y,\frac13-y)\ge\frac23\).  Splitting the larger piece \(\frac23\) into \(x\) and \(\frac23-x\) yields pieces \(x,\frac23-x,\frac13\).  The sum of the odd‑indexed pieces is \(1-\text{median}\).  The median is at most \(\frac13\) (achieved when the two pieces from the larger piece are equal), so the odd‑sum is at least \(\frac23\).  Thus in every case Liu obtains at least \(\frac23\), so \(c(1)\ge\frac23\).
